1855Founding & institutional
Rev. Elias Camp Morris — born
Born into slavery in Murray County, Georgia. Self-educated; worked as a shoemaker while developing as a preacher. Future co-founder of ABC and first president of the National Baptist Convention.
1860COGIC formation
Mother Lizzie Robinson — born
Future Matron of ABC and founder of the COGIC Women's Department. Her personal papers are held at the Flower Pentecostal Heritage Center, Springfield, Missouri.
1865COGIC formation
Bishop Charles Price Jones — born
Future ABC graduate, co-founder of COGIC, and founder of the Church of Christ (Holiness) U.S.A.
1866COGIC formation
Bishop Charles Harrison Mason — born
Born near Bartlett, Tennessee, son of former slaves. Future founder and First Senior Bishop of the Church of God in Christ — today the largest African American Pentecostal denomination in the world with 6.5 million members.
1879Founding & institutional
E.C. Morris — pastor, Centennial Baptist Church
Becomes pastor of Centennial Baptist Church in Helena, Arkansas, building the platform that leads to ABC's founding five years later.
1884Founding & institutional
Arkansas Baptist College — founded
Founded November 1884 as the Ministers' Institute at Mount Zion Baptist Church, Little Rock. Funded by the Colored Baptists of Arkansas. E.C. Morris serves as Chairman of the Board of Trustees.
1885Founding & institutional
Renamed Arkansas Baptist College
April 1885: institution officially renamed Arkansas Baptist College. The only Baptist-affiliated HBCU west of the Mississippi River.
1887Founding & institutional
Joseph Albert Booker — first president
One year after graduating from Roger Williams University, Booker is hired as ABC's first president. He serves for 39 years until his death in 1926.
1893COGIC formation
Bishop Mason — matriculates at ABC
Charles Harrison Mason enrolls at Arkansas Baptist College on November 1, 1893. He transfers to the Minister's Institute program and graduates in 1895.
1895Founding & institutional
E.C. Morris — first NBC president
Elected the first president of the National Baptist Convention — the largest denomination of Black Christians in America. Holds the position for 27 years. By 1900 the NBC represented over 60% of African American churchgoers.
1895COGIC formation
Mason graduates; meets Jones
Mason graduates from ABC's Minister's Institute. Jones, an ABC graduate, and Mason meet and become the primary architects of the Holiness movement among Black Baptists in the South.
1897COGIC formation
Mason receives the name "Church of God in Christ"
Walking a street in Little Rock near the ABC campus, Mason says God revealed the name of his future denomination, drawn from 1 Thessalonians 2:14.
1908Founding & institutional
E.C. Morris — emissary to the Belgian Congo
Appointed by President Theodore Roosevelt as personal emissary to the Belgian Congo to investigate reports of colonial atrocities.
1907–1910COGIC formation
Mason transforms COGIC after Azusa Street
After attending the Azusa Street Revival in Los Angeles (1907), Mason returns and organizes COGIC as Pentecostal. By 1910 he has ordained 300 white Pentecostal preachers, directly influencing the formation of the Assemblies of God.
1911COGIC formation
Mother Lizzie Robinson — founds COGIC Women's Department
After losing her ABC staff position upon receiving Spirit baptism, Robinson organizes the COGIC Women's Department. She founds the Prayer and Bible Band and lays the foundation for COGIC's Missions Department. "The globalization of COGIC came from Lizzie Robinson." — Rev. Elijah Hill, COGIC Historian
1913Distinguished alumni
E. Alice Taylor — graduates ABC
One of ABC's early female graduates. Relocates to Boston; founds a branch of Poro Beauty College in 1927 serving 150 students annually. Serves as an NAACP officer and board member for 50 years.
1914Distinguished alumni
Joseph Robert Booker — B.A., ABC
Son of the first ABC president. Completes law at Northwestern University (1917). Works alongside Thurgood Marshall on NAACP cases; serves as president of the National Bar Association (1949–1950).
1922Founding & institutional
Rev. E.C. Morris — dies
Dies in Little Rock on September 5, 1922. Co-founder of ABC and first NBC president. Centennial Baptist Church, his home congregation, is recognized as a National Historic Landmark in 2003.
1940McKissic family
James Henry "Jimmy" McKissic — born
Born to Rev. James E. and Rosa McKissic. His mother teaches him piano from age three until age thirteen. He goes on to perform at Carnegie Hall 28 times.
1945COGIC formation
Mason Temple dedicated; Robinson dies
Mason Temple dedicated in Memphis — the same building where Dr. King delivers his final "I've Been to the Mountaintop" sermon (1968). Mother Lizzie Robinson dies the same year, having served the COGIC Women's Department for 34 years.
1949Distinguished alumni
Bishop Donnie Lee Lindsey, Sr. — graduates ABC
WWII veteran who felt called to ministry during his service. Graduates ABC; elevated to COGIC Bishop (1974). First African American on the North Little Rock City Council. Inducted into the Arkansas Black Hall of Fame (2015).
c. 1950saddedDistinguished alumni
Rev. James S. Allen, Sr. — graduates ABC
Born 1936 in Columbia County, Arkansas. Pastors for 64 years across three states. In Philadelphia: helps break a 50-day teachers' strike (1981); co-founds the Black Clergy of Philadelphia, instrumental in electing the city's first Black mayor (1983); advises President Clinton. Vice President, National Baptist Convention USA, Inc.
1956McKissic family
Rev. William Dwight McKissic, Sr. — born
Son of Rev. James E. and Rosa McKissic. Future founder of Cornerstone Baptist Church, Arlington, Texas; lecturer at Harvard, Emory, and Wheaton; author of three books.
1961COGIC formation
Bishop Charles Harrison Mason — dies
Dies November 17 in Detroit, Michigan. Interred at Mason Temple. One of the most consequential figures in the history of American Christianity. COGIC now counts 6.5 million members and 12,000 congregations worldwide.
1969McKissic family
Jimmy McKissic — Hertz Scholarship
Receives the prestigious Hertz Scholarship to study in Geneva, Switzerland. Later serves as house pianist at the Hotel Martinez in Cannes, France (1984–2007).
c. 1970saddedDistinguished alumni
Rev. Jerry D. Black, Sr. — graduates ABC
ABC and Ouachita Baptist University graduate. Grows Greater Paradise Baptist Church (Little Rock) from 17 to 3,000 members. Moves to Beulah Missionary Baptist Church, Decatur, Georgia (1991), growing it to megachurch scale on a 40-acre campus. Honored with a U.S. postal stamp; Doctor of Humane Letters, ABC 2022.
c. 1970saddedDistinguished alumni
Rev. John Modest Miles — graduates ABC
Earns B.S. from Arkansas Baptist College. Serves 19 years as a Kansas City, Missouri Police Officer before entering full-time ministry (2013). Senior Pastor of Morning Star Baptist Church, Kansas City (since April 5, 1987). Previously served as an instructor at ABC.
c. 1970saddedDistinguished alumni
Rev. C. Dennis Edwards, I — ABC-connected
President, Consolidated Missionary Baptist State Convention of Arkansas; Pastor, St. John Missionary Baptist Church, Little Rock. Honored among CMBSC 50-year pastoral honorees in 2018. His ministry represents the deep institutional covenant between ABC and the Black Baptist community in Arkansas.
1978McKissic family
Rev. W.D. McKissic — graduates Ouachita Baptist University
Earns B.A. in Christian Studies. Later completes a Master of Theological Studies from Southwestern Baptist Theological Seminary.
c. 1980saddedDistinguished alumni
Dr. Clifton Conley — graduates ABC
Earns B.S. from ABC; later M.Div. from Virginia Union University. Returns to chair the Department of Religious Studies at ABC — the program elevated and renamed as the McKissic School of Christian Studies. One of the foundational architects of the school's academic infrastructure.
1983Distinguished alumni
Rev. Louis Edward Williams, Jr. — graduates ABC
Graduates with a degree in Christian Theology. Elected Pastor of Saint Bethel Missionary Baptist Church, Pine Bluff (1999). Earns a Bachelor of Theology from Saint Thomas Christian University at age 79 in 2021 — embodying ABC's founding conviction of lifelong learning.
1983McKissic family
Rev. W.D. McKissic — plants Cornerstone Baptist Church
Begins Cornerstone Baptist Church in Arlington, Texas, in his garage with 15 members. The church now averages approximately 1,800 in attendance on a 40-acre campus.
1986McKissic family
Jimmy McKissic — first Carnegie Hall appearance
First of 28 Carnegie Hall performances. The 1989 documentary How Do You Get to Carnegie Hall? documents his story. Inducted into the Arkansas Black Hall of Fame in 1994.
1997McKissic family
Rev. James E. McKissic — dies
Pastor of Mount Calvary Missionary Baptist Church, Pine Bluff, dies after decades of ministry. Known for setting aside the fourth Sunday each month to mentor younger preachers. The McKissic School now bears his name.
2010Distinguished alumni
Dr. Nathanael A. Palmer — graduates ABC, Summa Cum Laude
ABC alumnus, class of 2010. Earns M.Div. from Memphis Theological Seminary (2016, Summa Cum Laude) and D.Min. from Beeson Divinity School, Samford University (2021). Now serves as Associate Dean of the McKissic School and co-author of this Legacy Record.
2013McKissic family
Jimmy McKissic — dies
Dies February 13, 2013. Donates his entire music collection to UAPB. A scholarship at UAPB is established in his honor. His life embodies Rosa McKissic's investment in her son's calling.
2021Distinguished alumni
Arkansas Heritage marker erected for Bishop Mason
A 2021 Arkansas Heritage historical marker in downtown Little Rock confirms: "Mason first preached in Preston, Arkansas, and graduated from Arkansas Baptist College Minister's Institute." ABC's role in Mason's formation is formally documented in state history.
2022Founding & institutional
ABC awards Doctors of Humane Letters
Arkansas Baptist College awards the Doctor of Humane Letters to Rev. Jerry D. Black and to Rev. William Dwight McKissic, Sr., formally bonding the McKissic family name to the institution.
2026Founding & institutional
Department renamed — James & Rosa McKissic School of Christian Studies
The Department of Religious Studies is formally elevated and renamed the James & Rosa McKissic School of Christian Studies at Arkansas Baptist College — the culmination of 140+ years of covenant history, ministry formation, and HBCU legacy. Legacy Record authored by Rev. Henry L. Parker, Jr. and Dr. Nathanael A. Palmer.
